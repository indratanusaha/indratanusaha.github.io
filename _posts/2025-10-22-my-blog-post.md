## Beware ye of genAI complacency, lest you forget the core issue !

The age of AI slop in the name of entertainment (referring to the AI-powered Mahabharata TV show) has dawned upon us. This new iteration doesn't even try to innovate, it's only a random collection of 'let's take inspiration from some epic movies like Baahubali & Ponniyin Selvan duologies & some other highly rated historical movies & give those visuals the context of Mahabharata instead of understanding the story & then deciding the visual context', & BOOM!

There goes an epic about the full spectrum of human nature presented in all its heinous imperfection & tainted glory by the author through the characters of the epic reduced to JUST.A.VISUAL. that would momentarily sedate the eyes by its resolution but is actually hollow.

-----------------

The keyword is DECISION OF CREATIVE AUTONOMY. Why would a creator not want to decide which visual to use & in what context? Why would they forgo their agency in the storytelling process?

The main question is WHY?

Who needs another iteration of Mahabharata if it's not even human? Look at the trailer. Looks like a bunch of differently colour-graded frames inspired from Gladiator-esque movies & the Baahubali & Ponniyin Selvan movie duologies stitched together under a common banner.

How would they portray the intricate & conflicting humanity in the characters? Let's take an example of Pitamaha Bhishma. How will an AI-generated model of Bhishma portray the nuanced emotional core of leading the Kauravas against his favorite Pandavas, or how would that model portray how he didn't realize what he was avoiding in all the years of his life despite his capability in weapons & politics? How would that model portray the regret of it all coming down on him during his confrontation with Krishna?

This is just one example, & there are multiple characters outside of the black/white framework of literature. Will discuss more on dissecting humanity through the lens of the characters of Mahabharata in some other articles, maybe dissecting
1. How Krishna did some things differently than Bhishma & how they both were the opposite of each other in some very specific instances,
2. What was the difference between Karna & Arjuna, not just situationally, but in terms of internal dialogue, & how is Karna a cautionary tale to competent people under the illusion of self-sufficiency?

It's nuances like these & the refusal of the author Shri Ved Vyasa to let his characters be boxed into simplified morally good or bad areas is what makes Mahabharata a full-fledged case study on the intricacies of human nature, because of its multi-dimensional characters & world building. One could write a thesis on Mahabharata's timeless portrayal of humanity & it won't even cover a third of the story.

Maybe AI can convey those emotions better than humans at some point of time in the future, but it can't just yet, evidently. And even if it does, it'd still need to be trained on real human data.

Should we even be investing in AI-generated creation when AI can be used for much more urgent use cases? More on this in the next section.

-------------------

This isn't even the core issue. The core issue is of respect, resource allocation, & depletion. Our epics deserve to be respected with wholehearted efforts, not some repetition of trying to modify a prompt in AI over & over again.

How does the exorbitant consumption of water & electricity for generating this AI generated video series justify the mindless repetition retraining & retrying the non-existent visual flair, where areas like Pune & Bangalore & many others are facing water crisis over the years?

We should be investing this amount of money & resources in research & development of technology that has the potential to help generations who will come long after we're dead, & what are we doing? What's even the point of all this? Our water bodies are drying up due to climate change caused by a multitude of factors (more on this in some other post), cities are getting more & more polluted day by day, housing crisis is a headache for so many, & we're investing in..this?

We must resist this complacency & demand higher standards from us & our surroundings. Our epics, be it Ramayana, Mahabharata, or any instance from the Puranas or Upanishads, they deserve to be treated with respect. The country's & the world's resources must be used to advance humanity, not to shove mindless uncreative slop on it, & it is our responsibility as citizens to remind ourselves of the duty we have towards our nation & the world. Why is there another AI show being created when AI can be used in medical research, informed healthcare diagnostics, maybe improving manufacturing processes too if utilized correctly? How is that AI TV show even a breakthrough? It isn't just about doing something new, one can walk 2.5 kilometres instead of 2.4 & call it a breakthrough, but all that really is is an excuse for not trying hard enough. A breakthrough would be building up AI to a point where it could benefit humanity, not make more humans hooked to the screen for clickbait & views.

Now we must look at this from the lens of legacy, on how this reflects on those who sacrificed for us. Our ancestors didn't do back-breaking construction work for us to go back to the ways of 'human kill human to stay alive'. They didn't die for us to stay silent while witnessing the misallocation of time, funds, water, electricity & other resources in real time while the environment is getting destroyed by climate change. They didn't work jobs that they hated for us to die working jobs that we hate to buy things that we don't need to impress people that we don't care about. WE MUST WAKE UP. This is our world, for no other reason than the fact that we were born in it, & we have the individual responsibility to demand accountability from ourselves & from those working to take care of us in return for our blood, sweat & tears & the time we put in work. The individual responsibility can't be carried by another, same as the fact that every student has to write his own exam at school. We have the responsibility to stay accountable to put in our part of the work & ask questions responsibly.

-----------------

As I finish writing this post, I leave you with a question:

Why shouldn't we demand higher standards from ourselves? Why shouldn't we demand higher standards from the environment around us (not just physical, but digital too), even entertainment? And Mahabharata isn't just entertainment, it's our history that contains a vast ocean of knowledge for humanity, one that must not be traded for convenience.
