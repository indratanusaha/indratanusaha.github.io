## The unnatural restriction on Ātmā
The need for control on resources to extract profit from them via human beings is so restricting & suffocating for us because it is so unnatural, & feels precisely like that.

#### Why is it unnatural?

Because not only is it against nature (evidence: climate change), it is against human nature too.

When the self (the inner layer behind the body, some call it Ātmā) is dissatisfied, it destroys the outside world just to forget its own insignificance. It happened in Mahabharata specifically in the case of Duryodhana. Whether or not one believes it to be real is a different debate. In that destructive process, that dissatisfied self destroys its surroundings too, while those capable of changing the tide stay standing like Bhishma, Drona & Karna, on the sidelines, reveling in their self-imposed helplessness.

“The greatest evil is not doing wrong, but seeing wrong and doing nothing.” (Bhagavad Gita 3.24)

We humans, despite all the good & bad in our nature, are intrinsically wired to seek connection towards a higher purpose.

Capitalism, in its current stage, inverts that intrinsic wiring & tries to restrict it to seeking shelter for oneself & then for immediate family. And there goes our higher purpose—scraping away food from dustbins & sleeping on footpaths, working jobs that we hate to buy survival.

“If we have our own why in life, we shall get along with almost any how. Man does not strive for pleasure, only the Englishman does.” (Friedrich Nietzsche, Twilight of the Idols)

And the moment we are rendered incapable or too strong for the system, we are ejected from it.
Someone who speaks up for safety is fired from his job, not only because of perceived disobedience but to make an example of the truthful person, so that the rest comply in fear & abandon their truths.

Here's my answer to that false compliance.

श्रेयान्स्वधर्मो विगुणः परधर्मात्स्वनुष्ठितात्।
“Better one’s own duty imperfectly performed than another’s well executed.” (Bhagavad Gita 3.35)


## Narrative engineered towards becoming the next billionaire through “hard work”

We have been fed the dream of the “self-made man,” while the system itself ensures most remain exploited. The illusion of opportunity sidelined the reality of ownership of resources.

From the 1980s, we have been fed the idea of 'work hard & you'll be the next millionaire'. Accounting inflation, that means a billionaire now. The reality that every common person is closer to becoming homeless than a billionaire has been successfully sidelined by narrative engineering. This is a curious case of pretending that 'trickle-down economics' was working. (Read more here: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trickle-down_economics)
The truth is that trickle-down economics could never work, despite how genius it may have looked on the surface. The free market, as was termed, provided an infinite potential for growth, but the downside to the environment or to the people wasn't considered. The greed of those who once experienced lopsided economics under the guise of generating profit & employment & got benefits from it wasn't considered, & neither was their willingness to exploit the environment & workers for increasing shareholder profit & their own salaries considered. The result: top executive salaries got higher while those who worked & grinded till they were blue in the face kept working for the same wages, while inflation continued as the Federal Reserve continued adding more funds. And every common person has been paying the price ever since.

The average person today can't afford a home, examples: people in USA, Australia, UK, living in their cars or RVs.

We feel it, yet most of us still defend the very overlords who harm us. They're overlords only because we let them be. We've somehow distanced ourselves from our own lives & the people around us, & also because we falsely believe that if we comply, the whip of systemic injustice won't fall on us. That's where we're wrong. A serpent won't spare you just because you fed it. We stay silent where we should speak up. No religion will protect a person if he doesn't speak up for truth. In Mahabharata, Bhishma, Drona & Karna were all religious characters, & yet, they met their end because they facilitated adharma. Obviously, this section related to Mahabharata demands a specific essay in itself, but in short, the cost of those three competent characters staying silent was paid by the entire kingdom & countless soldiers.

“When you gaze into the abyss, the abyss gazes back. Whoever fights monsters should see to it that in the process he does not become a monster.” (Friedrich Nietzsche, Beyond Good and Evil)

However, as Nietzsche warned, we must not focus solely on the problem & let negativity rule us. We're still able-bodied & young, we can change the system & replace it with something fair. Our objective is to find solutions after identifying problems, not just shout at the problem hoping it'll be solved.


## When purpose lost to compliance

It is no mystery that a man's masculinity withers without a higher purpose. It has been proven time & again throughout history that a man is at his best alignment (internally & externally) when he's devoted or committed to something bigger than himself.

Devoid of a purpose, what does man do? He indulges in vices to fill the void inside him. Some spend lifetimes chasing a fantasy of a woman, some chase material success. None of these are wrong or right, these are empty.

"काम एष क्रोध एष रजोगुणसमुद्भवः।

महाशनो महापाप्मा विद्ध्येनमिह वैरिणम्॥"

“It is desire, born of rajas, that is the insatiable enemy of man.” (Bhagavad Gita 3.37)

For a man committed to a purpose, relationships, resources & materials become force multipliers, not chasms or mirages. 

For me, that purpose is Swarajya, not the one where flags are raised, but where every person can be self-sovereign, grow his own food, become part of a community, go back to villages & stay with his family & friends. Remember childhood? When our friends lived near each other? Vikings lived like that in adulthood too. It was called a settlement.

If we trace capitalism to its earliest roots, at first, some people from the villages came to the city for employment because their capital (land) was forcibly taken from them & then they had no choice but to sell their labour in exchange of meagre wages. That's what every colonizer did in history, capture capital (which was land back then), then kick out the natives & force them to work factory in exchange for meagre wages that somehow didn't cover food & shelter, all in service to the empire. The term was 'slavery' then. Has that truly changed now?
We've reached a point where villages (true village communities in spirit) are vanishing at an alarming rate, same as endangered animals. This is fueled by both the greed induced by late-stage capitalism reaching peaks & also the despicable ignorance of the common people.

## The felt truth

In our hearts, we know that money or relationship without a purpose is too small a thing to be a higher purpose. We instinctively reject barely surviving to be the highest purpose of life. That is precisely why the Independence movements throughout history had people from all echelons of society, because it gave people a chance to be a part of something bigger than themselves even though the odds were stacked against them. But at that time, the enemy had a face, the British in the context of India. Now our enemy is complacency & thinking that we'll sit through the climate change storm & the rampant destruction of our planet for greed. We can't. Our home is burning, our lungs are being filled with toxins & smoke, & we still miraculously hope for someone else to take the reigns? What has happened to the spirit of the youth?

At the very least, we must have the sensibility to decide what's healthy for us & discern the rest.
That's where the masterstroke of modern education system lies. Most of us don't know much about the bodies we inhabit, yet we call ourselves educated. Most of us can't determine when a person is speaking from his gut & when they're speaking from throat, yet we pat ourselves for scoring marks in a system designed to breed compliance. What use is this education when people can't even discern what's bad for them? But discussion of the education system from a few decades is a different topic, which I'll dissect in another post.

Humanity’s fall did not begin with greed alone, it began when we replaced purpose with profit, and forgot that to act without duty is to destroy what sustains us.

"उत्सीदेयुरिमे लोका न कुर्यां कर्म चेदहम्।
संकरस्य च कर्ता स्यामुपहन्यामिमाः प्रजाः॥" (Bhagavad Gita 3.24)
