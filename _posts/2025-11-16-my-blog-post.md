# rain without monsoon

It was an ordinary day in April.

Not all storms come in monsoon.

I got on the evening bus from work to my apartment, where I stayed in a small room.

While I was getting off the bus, it started raining heavily.
The bus driver, a known face, told me to open my umbrella & waited for me to get down.

My home was 300 meters from the bus stop.

Winds had started howling.

By the time I walked 50 metres, the umbrella had bent upside down, as my life was then. I had been drenched, my denim & blue t-shirt soaking wet.
I still kept walking. The winds weren’t ferocious enough to deter me from going to my room.

They weren’t ferocious enough, not yet.

I walked another 20 metres, & my backpack almost flew away. I held the rightmost metal pillar of the car-shed, & paused, holding it tightly with my left hand. The bent umbrella was in my right hand, my backpack was floating behind me, held only by the strong straps.

I was getting bored.

*‘how long will I waste time here getting drenched?’*

I had started feeling cold, and the water in my socks was making my feet feel heavy.

I had started feeling helpless, of being stuck forever, unwillingly. The storm outside had become indistinguishable from the storm inside.

I was waiting for the winds to slow down, & stared at the evening sky impatiently, asking,

*‘what’s the point? I still have to go to work tomorrow, don’t I? Then why would you waste my time?’*

Then came the twist, or the turning point. The term didn’t matter, in the face of this sheer absurdity.

The gale and rain together had turned into a ferocious storm.

The storm struck violently against my backpack & the right side of my body, & almost made me fly. I got slammed into the pillar of the car-shed, my left hand almost slipping from the support. I held tight, held on to life, against meaningless death, even though I had claimed earlier that I don’t love my life. Water, the life-sustaining element, was splashing on me from the right, & I couldn’t see a thing. I held on.

I had no idea how long I had been standing in the same position, holding on to the pillar. My fingers were going numb, my jaw was clenched. 10 minutes felt like an hour.

The next moment, the gale had slowed down ever so slightly. The rain had become less violent.

*‘this is the moment, let’s go now, finally!’*

I put my right foot forward. The gale had become ferocious again.

*‘not again, come on!’*

I felt the wind sweeping me away, took a step back & held the support, & waited. But this time, the feeling of wanting to be anywhere else had gone.

The gale had become merciless now. The rain had become louder.

*‘my notebooks would have become completely wet..’*

I wasn’t seeing a way out of the storm. I was drenched from my hair to my feet. I was standing with support from the metal pillar, couldn’t see a thing with water splashing on my face every moment, and my hand was slipping from the support.

*‘I think I might die today..’*
*‘Then so be it..’*
*‘But what if I don’t get to live again? I have so much that I put off, I thought I had tomorrow..’*
*‘You’d still go down fighting..’*
*‘But I didn’t choose this fight..’*
*‘No one does, the fight chooses its warriors. We can only answer the call, or not..’*
*‘But, I..I.. haven’t accomplished..doesn’t matter now. I’m going to die. Maybe this is what the people who live near cyclonic areas feel so often. I never imagined it to be this intense and overwhelming. It sure doesn’t seem this intense from TV. I now see how meaningless and absurd all this is, how fragile life is, and it means it is upto me to interpret it however I want to..’*
*‘But it is still absurd..’*
*‘Yes..’*

After perhaps 20 more minutes, the gale paused. I walked the rest of the distance, didn’t think of checking my clock after reaching my room. I changed, took a shower, made a sketch, wrote about this near-death experience in my diary, ate food, and slept like a baby.

The next day, a weight had vanished. My eyelids felt lighter, my breath deeper.

Perhaps I had become free of the future, and of the past.

Perhaps I had started living in the present.

Perhaps I had realized that all of life is absurd. Perhaps that absurdity is what makes space for it to hold meaning.
