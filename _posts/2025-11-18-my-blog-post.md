# India is protein deficient. It's a problem to fix.

## This Problem Is Civilizational, Not Just Nutritional

A weak & unhealthy population creates a weak nation. A weak nation cannot protect and preserve its sovereignty.

**Facts in Context:**
- Climate change has already amplified India’s nutritional crisis:
- Rising temperatures have reduced protein content in major crops like rice (global studies show a drop of 5–15% protein under elevated CO₂ conditions).[1]
- Erratic monsoons have reduced pulse yields & contributed to a significant increase in food inflation. In simpler terms, this means that the prices of pulses like Tuwar dal, which is a key protein source for many vegetarians, is becoming unaffordable.[2]
- Fish populations along the Indian coastline have declined & are switching migration patterns due to rising sea temperatures, coral bleaching & harmful algal bloom, all of which are related to climate change. Now that the overall fish production has increased in India manifolds, it is the right time to make it affordable for atleast the omnivore population. [3]

Climate change is attacking India’s protein supply directly.

This is why nutrition is not just health policy, it is climate policy, economic policy, and national security policy.

“दुर्बलस्य नास्ति मित्रम्” (Chanakya)

Translation: The weak have no allies.

**Public health consequences are visible:**
- 57% of Indian women and 25% of Indian men are anaemic(NFHS-5) aged 15-49. Although this does not seem related to protein diet, a lack of protein in diet often leads to lack of nutrients like iron & vitamin B12 too, which leads to anemia.[4,5]
- India has the world’s second largest population of diabetics (approx. 77 million). This may look unrelated to protein deficiency, but a lack of protein in diet leads to less fullness, which leads to sugar & junk-food cravings, which in turn leads to irregular blood sugar spikes.[6] “Experts agreed that increasing protein intake offers myriad health benefits, namely reducing glycemic variability, improving glycemic control, increasing insulin sensitivity, improvement in lipid profile and immunity, and helping in weight management and preservation of muscle mass in PWD.”[7]
- Sarcopenia (loss of muscle mass) is increasing both in the elderly and the youth.[8]

India is facing a paradox of low muscle mass along with high fat percentage, which looks like a pot belly, or skinny fat, & it has become a silent epidemic born of neglect.

## Why citizens’ overall health matters?

The health of a nation depends on the health of its citizens.

*What is health?*

By definition, *health is a state of complete physical, mental & social well-being, which includes the absence of diseases*[9], or steps towards the treatment of the same to ensure well-being. These three aspects of health are inter-connected, but the physical & mental element are more important than the social aspect in the current state of the world.

Physical and mental health rest on the culmination of consistency in proper nutrition, exercise & sleep, & all three factors are non-negotiable. Social health is the outer radiance of inner holistic strength, & it collapses when the foundation weakens.

*"All animals are equal, but some animals are more equal than others"* (George Orwell, Animal Farm)

## At present, why are physical & mental well-being more important than the social aspect?

If we look around, the social element has increasingly become more uncertain and unstable. Rising infidelity, surface-level access to many people, lack of depth in interactions, these are all factors, but beyond the scope of this essay.

Put succinctly, the narrative weight of individualism-fueled consumerism & the existential weight of (most) meaningless (performative) work of exploiting our one planet that can sustain life, until the last person is dead, is taking our social lives away, maybe not in an instant, but minute by minute, it's bleeding us dry until we're too fragmented to notice, & too weak to resist.

50-year mortgages?

15-year car loans?

Isn't that absurd?

Public health literature calls this *biopsychosocial factors of health*[10], & I am calling this an erosion of the same factors, where a system designed for extraction eventually extracts vitality.

In this vicious cycle, climate stress, debt cycles, weak nutrition, & social disadvantage reinforce one another. A protein-deficient nation moves into a fatigued state, physically and psychologically.

## Reclamation of sovereignty by prioritizing health

To reclaim oneself, the first step is to reclaim overall holistic health, and commit to strengthening those aspects of health which can be taken care of with discipline.

Mental & physical health are not mutually exclusive, both fuel each other. Social health improves, fueled by both.

In terms of physical health, muscle mass is important, & so is leanness upto a certain extent. Of these, muscle mass is more important from a purely functional angle. Increasing & maintaining muscle mass is simple, but not easy. One just has to weight train or do basic calisthenics movements regularly. That could range from doing exercises twice in a day to exercising once in a chosen interval of 1-4 days.

**Why is muscle mass important?**

It reverses aging. It reduces biological age, & preserves youth. It improves bone health, keeps the cartilages from drying up. Above all, it makes us fit enough to carry a month's groceries without breaking a sweat, irrespective of gender. 

Let's look at the facts:

- Muscle mass is a key predictor of both physical & mental longevity & resilience & lower mortality risk, according to many studies. [11,12]
- Even modest resistance training increases insulin sensitivity by over 40%, which reduces type 2 diabetes risk. [13]

**Why is leanness also important?**

A lower fat percentage ensures that the legs aren't overburdened, & a person stays agile in most cases. Obviously, there are many people who have a high body fat percentage & are still very agile, but only on that basis, the importance of leanness can't be dismissed. From an aesthetic lens, it improves the social aspect of health.

“शरीरमाद्यं खलु धर्मसाधनम्” (Kālidāsa)

Translation: The body is the primary instrument of all dharma.

Having & maintaining a healthy body isn't vanity, it is a necessity to embody dharma.

**Why is protein even important?**

For muscle mass maintenance & muscle synthesis, protein is required, & is one of the most important macronutrients in the diet. Protein is the building-block of muscles. Without enough protein, the body won't be able to build muscle optimally or maintain it, even with extremely well executed weight training. Carbohydrates is another important aspect for muscle mass along with protein, but since Indian diet already has abundance of carbohydrates, I don't find it necessary to write about that in this essay.

**The problem is defined**

But, the problem currently is that protein, which is the most essential macronutrient to build & maintain muscle mass, has become a privilege accessible only by the rich people, & the lack of access of other people is mostly due to a lack of emphasis & action taken to improve the percentage of protein in the Indian diet.

Having & maintaining decent muscle mass shouldn't be a privilege, but in the current economic state, it is. With inadequate protein in the diets of college students in India who eat in the hostel messes, it is no wonder that India is producing a majority of individuals with low muscle mass when they come out of college as 22-23 years old young adults.

This lack of implementation is accentuated by the lack of mainstream awareness about the importance of muscle mass in all age groups, since the parents of these students don't even demand from institutes to increase the protein content in the diets. Notice how mainstream awareness that is missing, because nutrition science & muscle mass & resistance training are all well-researched topics from more than two decades, but due to the lack of mainstream awareness, most common people don't even know whether rice contains protein or carbohydrates, & in what quantity.

I believe it is of utmost necessity that we atleast know the nutritional value & the calories that we eat. This is baseline general awareness.

Let's look at some facts:

- 73% of Indians consume less protein than needed (ICMR, 2023).[14,15]
- India ranks among the lowest per-capita protein consumers in Asia, however, recently, it has shown a slight upward trend.[16,17]
- Hostels and school meals consistently fail minimum thresholds of macronutrients, especially protein.
- ICMR has set the minimum protein requirement as 0.83 g/kg of bodyweight, but most of Indian population isn't close to that number.
- Japan, China, Russia, USA, all of these countries exceed not only Indian baseline protein demands, but they exceed their own standards in protein consumption too.

If colleges & schools were providing students adequate protein, it could've been dismissed as an awareness issue, but that isn't the case.

Hence, it is as much a policy implementation issue as it is an awareness issue.

**The current lack**

It is important to address where the lack is currently. A lack of awareness & an over-attachment to superstitions about diet doesn't help with physical health.

The recommended range of protein intake is 0.8-1.6 g/kg of bodyweight. The lower metric of 0.8 is recommended (RDA) only for sedentary adults, not active individuals.[18]

For young people, it is recommended that the protein intake is about 1.2-1.6 g/kg of bodyweight, or more, depending on what kind of daily activities they do. Exercise science varies on this, with some claiming that 2.2 g/kg is optimal. But that is a problem of those countries where protein is adequately involved in diet, not of India at present. An Indian diet doesn't consist of even 0.8 g/kg bodyweight of protein.

India consumes adequate calories, not adequate nutrition. This is why India is simultaneously thin, weak, anaemic, diabetic, protein-deficient, yet calorie-sufficient.

This is a paradox sustained not only by lack of awareness, but also by policy neglect.

**An example from college**

Let's consider a vegetarian food day in the hostel mess of a standard college.

Breakfast - idli, sambhar, sprouted chana, bread-jam, tea/milk

Lunch - dal, rice, salad (cucumber, tomato, onion, carrot), aloo matar, roti

Dinner - dal, rice, salad (same as lunch), aloo gobhi, roti

Calculating the protein: 1.6+5+10+12+6+12+6 = 52.6 g

An adult student would definitely weigh more than (52.6/1.2 =) 43.67 kg.

Even on the rare days chicken or paneer is cooked in the mess, the quantity is so low (3-4 small pieces on average) that the additional protein is hardly another 12 grams.

**This is where we must ask**

If we're feeding the youth meals of this low nutritional quality in the premier institutes of the country, after charging them lakhs of rupees for education & hostel combined, can we reasonably expect to produce strong individuals?

**Need of structural reform**

This is where structural reform is needed:

- Mandatory 25–30 g protein per meal in school & college hostels. Together, 3 meals would make it atleast 75 g protein, which is a good start.
    - Result: 5.9 million students living in hostels would become protein-sufficient.
- 0% GST on protein-rich foods for every citizen in the country. If diamonds can get favorable taxation, protein, which is the building-block of a nation, deserves better.
- Protein subsidy for every citizen in the country, for eggs, soy chunks, milk, curd.
- Weekly nutritional audits of institutional food quality which students can conduct.
- Every packaged food must list the following: Protein per 100g in bold,
Protein per serving in bold,
Percentage of Daily Requirement. Result: reform in behavior without coercion.
- Integration of protein-rich millets and pulses mandatorily in diets.
- Increase the production of drought-resistant millets in the wake of the impending danger of climate change.
- Educate about nutrition science from an early age, right from school. A child must know what he is eating, & why.

**A flickering hope**

But some portion of the youth, atleast the working professionals & college students are waking up, as evident from social media sites. But awareness is only the first step. The student still needs to be able to afford the protein. This is why awareness is only the initiation of improvement. The actionable part of making protein affordable still rests with the policymakers.

Even with awareness, the economic hurdle remains the same, the protein supplements do cut out a decent portion from the minimum wage in India (Rs. 783 being the lowest amount of minimum wage per day). Mathematically, buying protein supplements is possible on an Indian minimum wage, as the cheapest 1 kg protein (which lasts 1 month, is around 1800-2200 Rs.).

Practically, with all other expenses of rent, electricity, water, & family expenses, it seems more of a liability to a common man than a necessity, & this is where the lack of mainstream awareness plays a huge part, along with lack in policy implementation.

Therefore,

- Protein must be accessible through food ideally, not supplements.
- Policies must address affordability and availability of protein.
- State must treat protein as national infrastructure.

**The two-front challenge**

The challenge here is two front:

- Spread mainstream awareness about the necessity of protein in physical health, even without bodybuilding. Remove the superstition that protein is only necessary for bodybuilders.
- Make & reform policies so that the affordability of protein becomes of the same level as the other carbohydrate-heavy diets.

We can do better by creating a detailed nutrition chart of the menu in colleges & treating essential macronutrients like protein as non-negotiables. This can be implemented by students themselves. This only needs a policy reform at the level of government comprising of a fixed number of grams of protein as minimum, & the students can aid in the implementation effectively, as most hostels have student leaders.

SDGs 2 (Zero Hunger), 3 (Health & Well-being), 13 (Climate action), all align with this goal.

**History Lesson from Colonial Times**

Now that we've established that the current dietary system needs a reform, let us look at the history of how India came to be a protein-deficient nation in the first place.

It started with the British East India Company & their perverse need to exploit the Indian nature, ecosystem & people for their profits.

They stopped the production of high fibre crops like millets & forcefully shifted the agriculture to rice & wheat, which would provide higher calorie count & serve as better 'products' to sell.

What did they lose? Nothing, and made profit.

What did we lose? Nutritional uniqueness & sovereignty.

- The British East India Company occupied India, later continued by the British Colonial Government, used it & gained profit via indigo, cotton, rice, wheat, while bringing down India from 23% of the world's GDP to 4%.
- The Bengal Famines of 1770 & 1943 were caused by their prioritization of corporate profit over people, & no amount of pretty words or polished corporate tone can justify the mishaps. The Bengal Famine of 1770 killed approx. 30 million people, about 1/3 of the then current population, & that is not a case of mismanagement only. It is a problem of values. And when it got repeated again in 1943, the inference was crystal clear, this was a case of different prioritization of values.
- That kind of an event changes some patterns evolutionarily. In scientific language it is called epigenetics. The fat storage patterns of the body changed to ensure survival (lower metabolic rates, more fat storage to ensure longer survival) which were accentuated by the lack of protein at the time of British Raj & that is continuing till today.[19]
- British Raj needed Indians weak & compliant to coerce them & simultaneously, they feared awakening the full potential of the Indian population, because of their history with India's rebels. So, they adopted a much more perverse ideology of breaking the population from the inside. While the British elites feasted on meat, fish & eggs, common Indians (not zamindars or sahukars) had to make do with rice, dal & roti on good days when they could afford food. And the rest of the time, many went hungry for days.
- Colonial agricultural redesign broke protein diversity, crop resilience, nutritional continuity, culinary heritage, biological potential of generations.
- There were many instances of ryots refusing to grow Indigo in the areas of Bengal & Bihar, as indigo after harvesting made the soil unsuitable for rice, because indigo seeped far deeper into the roots of the soul than rice, affecting its fertility. British Raj needed only the harvest, but India & her children bore the cost.

Despite of these historical facts, the truth is that we don't have to carry that suffering in today's times. We must reclaim sovereignty in nutrition.
