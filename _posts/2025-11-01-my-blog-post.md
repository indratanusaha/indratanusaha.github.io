# The heart-wrenching case against Capitalism: Part 1 - The Cries of a Wounded Earth

“Man is a rope stretched between the animal and the Overman.”
(Friedrich Nietzsche, Thus Spoke Zarathustra)

The case against capitalism is absolutely essential for our times yet heart-wrenching.
It's the realization that many of the formative experiences we've had were all part of the same system & we still found joy in it. Be it the moment our fathers brought fruits for us without asking, or our mothers brought some toy without asking, all of those originated from the same system, as well as the lack of resources that led to them sacrificing their wants in the first place. This is a good example of a loving middle-class childhood, but not a poor one.

“Man, the bravest animal and most prone to suffer, does not deny suffering as such: he wills it, he even seeks it out, provided he is shown a meaning for it, a purpose of suffering.” (Friedrich Nietzsche, The Genealogy of Morals)

This realization that our parents’ suffering within an unjust & oppressive system were acts of meaning arising out of love for their children, not submission. The realization that no matter how many memories are associated with an oppressive system, if it doesn't benefit the common people, we must change it.

The motivation of a financial void is unnatural for humans. Internally, we're wired to be in groups & become a part in it, or lead it, but that's still being a part of the group. There's no leader without a group, but there can be a group without a leader if everyone in the group becomes a sustaining participant, where they won't need to be strung along by a fragile thread.

The realisation that someone else who was poorer than us suffered for even existing in his childhood is a staggering one, one that should ideally be enough for youth to reject the system & create a new one. But that isn't the case. We've become numb & desensitized & we can't feel the pain of those starving or dying unless faced with the same. We can't feel anymore. We can't participate in the shared consciousness, the kind that was written of in Mahabharata, specifically Gita.
"Unless every person in the world is satisfied (or happy), one can't be happy, & therefore, must strive to become a medium through which he can bring about necessary change."

विद्याविनयसम्पन्ने ब्राह्मणे गवि हस्तिनि।
शुनि चैव श्वपाके च पण्डिता: समदर्शिन:॥ (Bhagavad Gita 5.18)

Translation: the wise see the same in a learned man, a cow, an elephant, a dog, and even a dog-eater.

Meaning: the wise (here it implies aware) don't distinguish themselves from the world, because they understand that distinguishing themselves from the world is an act of ego-preservation, to subscribe to the belief that an individual's needs or wants are separate & above the collective consciousness, which is not true, unless that individual is the only one on the path of dharma. But then, that person would try to turn everyone towards dharma instead of trying to get something out of people. That is a marker for genuine intent.


## Our current tragedy & the will to rise outside of a meaningless & self-destructive system
Our tragedy is that we feel too much, so we numb ourselves by consumerism, & then we're left with nothing to preserve or protect. That is the great depression of our lives. A lack of worthy purpose. We realized that none of the jobs or things we do won't matter in a thousand years, & that realisation terrifies us, so much that we want to make a mark even at the cost of sacrificing the world & the people in it.
Instead of letting it be liberation, we let ourselves be chained by the fact of our insignificance. Instead of that being a joy, we let it fester into a wounded sorrow. And that binds us to the effect more than our action.

"The struggle itself toward the heights is enough to fill a man's heart." (Albert Camus, The Myth of Sisyphus)


## How & why do we numb ourselves to the suffering of the people in the world?
We assume they deserve it. Work hard, get a job, slog for 40 years, all a lie that was sold to us, & we're waking up, that's why the dissonance arises. But here's how we numb ourselves further: Instead of questioning the system which propagates the suffering of most for a handful to thrive, we question the individual who suffers & until we find a confirmation bias within his answer which somehow always comes to 'you don't work hard enough', 'tough luck but this is how the world works, take it or leave it', we don't assume credibility of his answer. Why don't we question the system? Because deep down, we've become so terrified of the chaos of freedom that we'd rather try to rise in a system that promises crumbs while breaking us apart, rather than creating a new system in which people won't have to apologize for existing.


सर्वारम्भा हि दोषेण धूमेनाग्निरिवावृता:॥ (Bhagavad Gita 18.48)

Translation: Every action is tainted with some imperfection, just as fire is covered by smoke.

Meaning: even flawed action for change is better than inaction in the name of waiting for perfection.
It is the same Swarajya (स्वराज्य) Shivaji envisioned, to have a dignified living for all. And it's also the same systemic change Krishna fought for. Bhagat Singh's revolution didn't stop at independence, he had a much larger vision of equity for people. That vision didn't die, people's faith in it died, or rather, that vision couldn't sustain itself without an anchor.

The next aim in this context should be to make precedents for a self-sustaining system. That is easier said than done. 

## Examining Communism as an alternative
Communism isn't an answer either. Let's take an example. If hypothetically, a Central Bank Digital Currency (CBDC) system is implemented worldwide, it would give the state (government) complete control over the micro things of a person's life. Ideologically, that surveillance isn't bad when isolated out of context. But, the delays associated with any level of bureaucracy & paperwork isn't a mystery anymore. That would render interventions useless in extremely urgent situations involving healthcare & urgent funds withdrawal or deposition. And another question is 'who defines the need?'

In a twisted way, this implementation would give complete control to the state regarding all resources & actions of the people, & it would be defended saying 'if you have nothing to hide, what do you fear?'. The fear & doubts aren't due to hiding, it's due to repeated mishandling of essential resources, misallocation & a lack of boundaries for the people who serve these billionaires. In spirit, this would be communism in terms of centralization, but the control would be in the hands of the technology owners & banking systems instead of the state. That's the twist. And with weapons of mass destruction at their disposal, the data of the entire human population & robots at their disposal, every man-made disaster would be dwarved compared to this one that's in the making, & this will only be accelerated by climate change.

## Karṇa & Kṛṣṇa are two sides of the same coin: Mahābhārata's warning

The distinction between Krishna & Karṇa is a simple one. Karna sought recognition in the same system that denied him the right to fight for himself even though he had the skills, citing his caste which was out of his control. Krishna dismantled & replaced the system that sought to undermine him. Both of them were given recognition by the system, only one chose to look beyond his personal gain & towards collective welfare. That was because Krishna was more in tune with the natural order & chaos of the universe, even if we remove the divine aspect from his being & consider him just a character in the story.
Karṇa wanted to rise through the oppressive system & he did rise in it, but at the cost of his own conscience & dharma. His mistake was to demand justice from an unjust system despite seeing & realizing the injustice of the system. That's why he is so tragic. He represents the part of ourselves that knows something better must replace the existing, yet is chained to his fear of feeling helpless, so much that his need for being seen as powerful overrides his inner truth. Kṛṣṇa, despite of being called many names & mocked by many, had the guts to question the system that was protected by stalwarts like Droṇa & Bhishma. This doesn't reduce Karṇa's importance, Karṇa also questioned the system, but he stopped after he got recognition & a spoon of seemingly equal rights for himself. That was the first step, & Karna never went beyond that. Kṛṣṇa went beyond & that ultimately led to the Kurukshetra war. This distinction is a topic that demands more detail & thorough analysis, will do that in another essay. If it reflects anything at all, it would be the author's brilliance in carving out two entirely distinct characters from one scaffold.

# Current trajectory & the possibility of waking up from ignorance
Think of the visual of the black mirror episode 'fifteen million merits'. We're in parallel to that, our existence commodified from birth to death. Yeah, that's right, if someone dies, their family has to pay for the last rites be it any religion. We dream of having retirement money in place but everyday the threshold goes up. We can pretend that only if we buckle hard & grind throughout our lives we'd be finally well off, but that illusion is falling apart. And once the truth has been seen, a creature can't unsee it. That's where the masterstroke of social media & tech-fueled motivation is. It distracts us from the soothing music of daily lives to the violently induced emotions of the algorithm. We're painfully coming to the fact that our world isn't what we thought it was, a straightforward equation of effort & reward, & we're also seeing that the system is failing us. As is obvious, change is chaotic. But the cost of not changing would be paid by us too. There won't be generations after us to pay it. Leaving ideology aside & looking at just practicality, we won't have anything to protect in 4 years time, & this isn't a country specific problem, it's a wound that has been allowed to fester for too long, & now when the vultures are circling it, we're waking up, cuz we'd been pretending to be carcasses, but our truth is that we're alive.
